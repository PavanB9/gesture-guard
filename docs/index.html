Three detectors

What it catches

Each detector has to see the behaviour hold for a moment before it fires, so a blink or a scratch doesn't trip it. One sensitivity slider tunes all three.

Yawning

A sustained wide-open mouth. Measured as a mouth aspect ratio from the face mesh, so it scales with how close you sit.

Signal MARHolds 1.5s

Face touching

A hand lingering near your nose or eyes — grooming, rubbing, nose-picking. Distance is measured relative to your face width.

Signal hand→faceHolds 0.45s

Background intrusion

A second person appears in frame behind you. Useful when you work somewhere with roommates, family, or foot traffic.

Signal face countHolds 0.6s

Local by construction

Nothing leaves your machine.

This is a privacy tool, so it doesn't get to make exceptions. The app window captures your camera and passes frames to an engine running on localhost. The guarded frame comes straight back. There is no server to send it to.

  • No cloud processing, no account, no sign-in
  • No telemetry or analytics of any kind
  • Video is never written to disk
  • Open source — read every line before you trust it

Works with your call app

Guard the feed Zoom actually receives.

Turn on the virtual camera and Gesture Guard publishes the already-protected stream as a webcam. Your call app never sees the raw feed — only the guarded one.

  1. Install OBS Studio once. You never have to open it — Gesture Guard drives the virtual camera device directly.
  2. Switch on Virtual camera in the dashboard's Output section.
  3. In Zoom, Teams, or Meet, pick OBS Virtual Camera as your camera.

Tested on Windows. If no driver is present the toggle explains why and everything else keeps working.
